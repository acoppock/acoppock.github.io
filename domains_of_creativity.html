Kaufman Domains of Creativity Scale

K-DOCS — 50 items, about ten minutes

Creativity is not one thing. It shows up differently in solving a problem with a friend, in building an argument, in performing, in taking a machine apart, and in making a picture. The questions below ask you to rate yourself in five of those areas separately, so what you get back is a profile of how you see yourself rather than a verdict about you.

Before you answer anything, guess your own profile. The comparison between what you expect and what you report is the point of the exercise, and it only works if you commit to a guess first.

Your answers are scored in your browser. Nothing is uploaded or stored anywhere, and closing the page erases everything.

Step 1 of 3: predict your profile

Compared to people of about your age and life experience, where do you think you fall in each domain? Move each slider before continuing.

1 = much less creative · 3 = neither more nor less creative · 5 = much more creative

A 5 means you would put yourself at the very top on every activity in that area, and a 1 means the very bottom on all of them. Pinning a slider to either end forces the later comparison to come out one way, so use the extremes only when you mean them.

Each score is the average of that domain's items, on the same 1 to 5 scale as the questions. Domains are not directly comparable to each other in the sense of "higher is better"; the shape across domains is what carries the information.

Where you were surprised

Write a short response to these before class.
  1. Which domain did you underestimate by the most? Why do you think you did?
  2. Your lowest domain is not a report on your potential; it is a report on what you have practiced. Which low domain would you most want to move, and what would practising it actually look like this term?
  3. Look at the Self/Everyday items. Many people who say "I'm not creative" score high there. Did you? What does that tell you about the definition of creativity you were carrying?
  4. The scale asks you to compare yourself to others. Would your answers change if it asked you to compare yourself to yourself a year ago?

What you just measured

Every item asked how creative you would rate yourself. So what came back is your own sense of what you can do, which is not the same thing as what you can make. The two come apart all the time, in both directions: people talk themselves out of domains they would be good at, and people overrate domains they have never tested. Treat the profile as a description of your beliefs about yourself, and treat the gap between it and your prediction as the interesting part.
